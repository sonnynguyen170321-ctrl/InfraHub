**Classification:** CURRENT_CANONICAL
**Pinned:** 2026-09-06
**Baseline production SHA:** `768610c56b73e55a9bed60c43ebd8bb5e41017aa`

> This is the operating brief for the InfraHub identity-led redesign. It supersedes ad-hoc
> visual direction. Where it conflicts with claims governance, owner-approved facts,
> accessibility, or measured performance, **business truth and accessibility win** (see §6).

## Purpose

The target is not "improve the current website." The target is a digital identity and website
experience that could credibly be sold as a **$50,000 premium infrastructure brand and website
engagement**, with the **InfraHub identity/logo system strong enough to stand as a $5,000+
identity deliverable on its own**.

The current implementation has strong engineering, accessibility, truth-governance,
progressive enhancement, partner provenance, and technical content. Do not throw that work
away. The weakness is perceived value: brand distinctiveness, art direction, typography,
composition, motion language, image quality, and ruthless editorial hierarchy.

The project operates as an **identity-led redesign on top of a proven technical foundation**.

---

# 1. Mission

Create a brand and digital experience that makes InfraHub feel:

- technically authoritative
- commercially trustworthy
- selective rather than marketplace-like
- globally credible
- physically grounded in real infrastructure
- premium without pretending to be a luxury fashion brand
- modern without looking like generic SaaS
- visually ownable at a glance
- calm, intentional, and confident
- memorable after one visit

The final product should feel appropriate in the same commercial conversation as major
infrastructure, network, data-center, cloud, and engineering brands while clearly remaining
InfraHub. Do not copy those brands. The point is to meet their level of identity discipline.

# 4. Core Brand Thesis

Do not build the brand around the generic "hub = center dot with lines." That territory is
oversaturated in IT, networking, cybersecurity, logistics, blockchain, cloud, and SaaS.

The stronger conceptual territory:

> **InfraHub makes the infrastructure reality underneath a requirement visible.**

Supporting ideas:

- a logical route is not always a physical route
- two suppliers can share one dependency
- one requirement can have several technically valid paths
- the correct provider depends on workload, location, capacity, resilience, timing, and
  commercial constraints
- InfraHub sits at the decision point between requirement and specialist delivery
- the value is not "having lots of products"; it is knowing what should be compared and why

Working internal brand statement:

> **From requirement to infrastructure reality.**

Not automatically the public tagline. It is the conceptual filter for identity, diagrams,
motion, layouts, photography, and content.

The goal is **system discipline**, not surface style.

# 8. Logo / Identity Sprint

Happens before the homepage redesign is finalized. Do not "improve" the current
cross-and-node logo. Treat the current mark as temporary.

## 8.1 Research / collision audit

Create a visual sheet containing the current InfraHub logo, 20-30 relevant
network/cloud/infrastructure/connectivity marks, and 20 generic "hub / network node /
connectivity" logos from the broader market, with notes identifying saturated visual tropes.

Explicitly screen out: center dot with four spokes; generic connected nodes; cloud outlines;
globe/network mesh; shield + network combination; infinity loops with no direct meaning;
generic hexagon; circuit-board monograms; random gradient ribbon; signal waves copied from
telecom conventions; arrows that look like logistics brands.

This is a visual collision screen, not legal trademark clearance. Record likely collisions
and avoid them.

## 8.2 Concept territories

Develop at least four genuinely different territories.

**Territory A — Hidden Dependency.** Two apparently separate paths reveal a shared underlying
segment or structure. Meaning: route reality, evidence below the logical layer, hidden
infrastructure dependency.

**Territory B — IH Infrastructure Monogram.** Build `I` and `H` from measured structural
geometry. Meaning: InfraHub ownership, architecture, physical structure, precise junctions.
The monogram must not simply be an "I" next to an "H" in a box.

**Territory C — Handoff / Interface.** Two systems meet through one intentional connection or
boundary. Meaning: requirement to provider, sourcing to specialist, technical handoff,
controlled introduction.

**Territory D — Logical / Physical Layers.** One symbol changes when a layer is shifted,
cropped, or revealed. Meaning: what the buyer sees, what exists underneath, InfraHub's
evaluative role.

A fifth wildcard concept may be explored if research uncovers a better ownable metaphor.

## 8.3 Sketch volume

Produce 20+ monochrome rough marks, reduce to 8, refine to 4, build 3 full identity boards,
select 2 finalists, recommend 1. Do not choose a winner because it looks cool at 400px.

## 8.4 Full identity board requirement

Each final territory must show: primary symbol; horizontal lockup; stacked/compact lockup;
custom wordmark treatment; monochrome version; reverse version; favicon / 16px micro-mark;
24-28px nav application; 64px social/avatar application; website hero application; technical
diagram application; partner proposal / document cover; simple physical or signage
application; motion concept; construction logic; clearspace; minimum size.

Use the Brandkit skill for boards. AI-generated concept images may inform art direction, but
the final logo geometry must be rebuilt as clean vector geometry and inspected manually.

## 8.5 Wordmark

Must not remain plain IBM Plex text with "Hub" colored blue. Explore custom `I`, custom `H`,
shared stem logic, precise cuts, optical corrections, subtle route/connection behavior,
custom kerning. Keep it restrained. Do not turn the wordmark into a puzzle.

## 8.6 Identity tests

Every finalist must pass: recognizable at 16px; recognizable in black only; recognizable in
white only; works without color; works without a rounded-square container; works on light and
dark backgrounds; survives laser engraving / emboss-style single-color use; survives 1-color
proposal printing; readable at header size; does not resemble a crypto token; does not
resemble a generic app icon; not dependent on gradients; not dependent on animation; symbol
is meaningful without an explanation paragraph; wordmark still looks premium without symbol.

## 8.7 Logo motion

Motion must derive from the identity concept — hidden route aligns; two layers resolve; one
junction reveals its dependency; one path hands off into another.

No spinning, floating, bouncing, perpetual pulsing, glowing, particle assembly, or generic
"draw every SVG stroke" animation unless the mark concept genuinely requires it.

Target: one restrained authored motion, normally 600-1100ms.

# 9. Typography System

IBM Plex is strong technical typography but currently makes the brand feel utilitarian. Do
not automatically discard Plex Mono; it can remain useful for real technical labels.

Create three licensed typography directions and test each on real pages. Each contains a
distinctive display/brand face, a highly readable body/UI face, and a technical mono.

Requirements: licensing documented; web performance measured; no synthetic weights; real
italics if used; display headlines feel owned; body remains clear for technical copy;
technical labels do not dominate; works at 390px mobile and 1920px desktop; wordmark
typography is not just the site font typed out.

Avoid defaulting to Inter, Roboto, Arial, Open Sans, or a trendy display font used merely
because it looks "premium."

Test on: hero H1; service-family H1; long technical article; partner profile; CTA;
table/technical annotation; route-diversity labels; header wordmark.

# 12. Homepage Strategy

Shorter, more confident, more memorable. Preserve content intelligence; move much of the UI
to deeper pages. Five major acts.

## Act 01 — Arrival / Brand Thesis

Establish InfraHub as a premium infrastructure sourcing and advisory brand within one
viewport.

Requirements: new identity; new typography; exceptional image/visual environment; concise H1;
concise supporting copy; one primary CTA; one secondary text action at most; signature
identity-linked motion.

No mini dashboard, no partner logo swarm over the hero, no badges, no fake metrics, no
decorative 3D object unless the identity system clearly justifies it.

The opening should feel authored even if motion is disabled.

### Entrance motion target

The current entrance feels fast to the owner. Build a more deliberate sequence. Suggested
desktop rhythm to prototype:

| Window | Event |
|---|---|
| 0-300ms | environment settles / exposure establishes |
| 250-650ms | mark or spatial brand cue resolves |
| 500-1100ms | headline appears |
| 850-1350ms | supporting line appears |
| 1100-1550ms | CTA resolves |

Do not blindly implement these numbers. Record the first 2.5 seconds and compare variants.
The whole sequence should feel calm, not sluggish.

## Act 02 — Credibility / Ecosystem

Prove InfraHub can connect the requirement to credible specialists. Use only currently
approved/public partner records.

Canonical confirmed list from the current claims register: FastNetMon, Gcore, StormWall,
Zenlayer, IPXO, Vates, ITcare, Airframe.

Official asset sourcing is not the same as permission to display a logo. Respect
`logoApproved`, public naming, and provenance gates. The wider owner inventory contains 21
candidates — internal opportunity records, not automatic public partners. Do not publish
unconfirmed candidates.

Prefer a calm selected-ecosystem composition with role/domain context and a clear distinction
between InfraHub and provider delivery. Do not make the homepage feel like a coupon
marketplace or reseller directory. The current continuous marquee may remain only if it
visually improves the final composition; a high-end static or controlled editorial
arrangement may be stronger.

## Act 03 — Method / Decision Framework

Explain why InfraHub is useful, using the real operating model:

**Understand → Match → Introduce → Deliver**

Show the six real qualification factors: workload, location, capacity, resilience, timing,
commercial constraints. Do not present all six as small dashboard buttons by default.

Explore editorial/spatial expressions: one architectural diagram transformed by the selected
factor; a single scrolling decision field; large typographic pairings; revealing
relationships rather than mini cards.

Preserve intelligence. Remove interface furniture.

## Act 04 — Proof / Signature Expertise

The route-diversity experience is the strongest existing signature content. Make it a
flagship brand experience.

Core lesson: logical diversity does not necessarily equal physical diversity.

Preserve: logical/physical distinction; shared dependency; named risk points; explanatory
text fallback; reduced motion behavior; keyboard accessibility.

Improve: diagram scale; composition; typography; spatial clarity; fewer visible controls;
less persistent pulsing; more authored state transitions; stronger relationship to new
logo/identity geometry.

If illustrative carrier names / ASNs can be mistaken for real carriers, replace them with
clearly fictional/generic labeling unless a factual reason exists to retain real identifiers.

Should feel like premium engineering editorial, not a monitoring dashboard.

## Act 05 — Conversation

Convert without clutter. Headline direction "Tell us what you're trying to build." remains
strong. Move detailed service selection to the inquiry page.

Homepage CTA: statement; one short supporting paragraph; primary action; one secondary
informational link if necessary. No four-chip selector box unless testing proves it
materially improves conversion without reducing premium perception.

# 15. Service-Family Page Template

Current family pages rely too heavily on equal cards. Redesign around an editorial decision
journey:

1. **Opening statement** — category, problem/requirement, concise summary, one strong visual
2. **When this family is relevant** — 3-5 real situations, not cards unless content requires
3. **What needs to be established first** — requirement / decision factors
4. **Available approaches** — child services, clear differences, no unsupported claims
5. **What to compare** — evidence and questions, technical risks, commercial considerations
6. **Relevant specialists** — only approved partners, why each may be relevant, no claim of
   exclusivity/strategy unless approved
7. **How InfraHub is involved** — sourcing / advisory / introduction / hardware transaction
8. **Technical insight / proof** — article or diagram
9. **CTA**

Avoid repeating the exact same visual grid on all five family pages. Maintain one system but
allow each family a distinct hero material: compute = metal/chassis/facility; cloud = compute
topology/hypervisor layers; network = optical path/fibre; security = traffic/routing/
mitigation architecture; operations = incident/handoff/NOC process.

# 20. Motion System

Motion is brand behavior, not decoration.

**Entry** — content resolve; controlled masked/clip movement; no exaggerated travel.
**Route** — lines reveal only when information changes; no random continuous motion.
**Layer transformation** — logical → physical; requirement → provider; hidden dependency →
revealed dependency.
**Image** — restrained crop/exposure changes; no default zoom-on-every-hover.
**UI** — press response; active indicator travel; arrow movement; tonal border/background
response.

Rules: no scroll hijacking; no custom cursor; no magnetic buttons; no excessive parallax; no
constant floating; no "every section fades up the same way"; reduced motion must feel
intentionally designed; motion must never imply factual certainty or performance.

---

# 21. 3D / WebGL Policy

Do not use 3D merely because the budget is high. 3D must earn visual meaning, brand
uniqueness, performance cost, accessibility fallback, and maintenance cost.

Prefer SVG, CSS transforms, high-resolution imagery, video only where art-directed, and
lightweight canvas for specific spatial effects.

If WebGL is proposed: prototype first; compare against SVG/pre-rendered alternative; measure
JS; measure parse cost; measure LCP impact; test no-WebGL fallback; test mobile; test reduced
motion; document why it is better.

Do not repeat the prior "decorative hardware object" experiment unless the new brand identity
makes a physical object genuinely central to the concept.

# 23. Performance Targets

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| LCP | under 2.5s throttled, aim under 2.2s |
| CLS | under 0.05 |
| INP | responsive, no heavy interaction thread blocking |
| Long tasks | none over 200ms in normal landing-page use |

Font payload intentionally budgeted; no third-party font runtime request; hero image
responsive and high resolution; below-fold images lazy-loaded; no giant autoplay video on
mobile; no uncontrolled animation loop; no WebGL on mobile unless proven necessary.

If creative work breaks performance, redesign the creative work. Do not "fix" a bad heavy
idea by throwing loading spinners at it.

---

# 24. Accessibility

Target WCAG 2.2 AA.

Semantic heading hierarchy; heading styling decoupled from tag level; visible focus; keyboard
controls; 44px-ish practical touch targets where relevant; reduced-motion behavior; high
contrast; text alternatives; actual buttons for actions; links for navigation; route diagrams
with equivalent document text; no inaccessible canvas-only explanation; no hover-only
critical information; screen-reader state updates for interactive diagrams; reasonable
zoom/reflow.

Fix the known "heading tag controls appearance" coupling during component-system cleanup.

# 31. "Looks Expensive" Acceptance Test

Before release, ask independent reviewers to look at the site for 10 seconds and answer:

- What does this company do?
- What do you remember visually?
- Does it feel like a reseller, SaaS template, consultancy, infrastructure operator, or
  something more specific?
- Does the logo look custom?
- Does the company feel trustworthy enough for a major infrastructure decision?
- What is the one signature idea?
- Does anything feel generated or templated?
- Does anything look over-designed?
- What part looks cheapest?
- What would you remove?

If reviewers cannot recall the mark or brand behavior, identity work is not finished.
